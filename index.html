I'm an Industrial and Operations Engineering student at the University of Michigan. This page collects things I've designed and built, mostly in Fusion 360, along with a couple of papers I've written.

Projects

01

Drill Press Vice Model

Fusion 360, motion joints, stress simulation

I modeled a drill press vice at full 1:1 scale in Fusion 360, working from a set of engineering design sheets. It has more than 20 components, all assembled with working motion joints, and I ran stress simulations on it to see how it would handle load. Getting the dimensions right across that many parts was the hardest part. Small errors added up, and once the tolerances stopped lining up I had to rebuild several components from scratch.

02

CNC Sign Design

Fusion 360, CNC toolpathing

This was my first project with CNC machining. I designed a wooden nameplate sign and had to program the toolpath on my own, which I'd never done before. After a lot of trial and error I landed on a mix of adaptive clearing and 3D contour passes. The design changed along the way too. Once I started modeling, I could tell my original sketch wasn't going to work on the router, so I redesigned it and machined the new version.

03

Workshop Small-Parts Organizer

Fusion 360, 3D printing (PLA)

I walked around my classroom's workshop looking for a problem worth solving and kept seeing small parts left scattered across the worktables with nowhere to go. I designed an organizer that clips onto a table corner, modeled it in Fusion 360, and printed it in PLA. It's still in use in my own room.

04

Cardboard Chair Design

Partner project · Fusion 360, anthropometric data, load testing

My partner and I wanted to see how much weight a chair made only of cardboard could hold. Before we modeled anything we took measurements from classmates, so the chair would fit an average student and not just look right on screen. We modeled it in Fusion 360 and split the build, with one of us cutting and the other assembling. In the load test it held 450 lbs before it gave out.

05

Study Divider Design

Fusion 360, user research, iterative prototyping

Studying in shared spaces is hard for me, so I set out to design something that helps. I surveyed AP and IB students at my school first, and nearly three quarters said they're often or always distracted when they study. The products already out there were too expensive, too bulky to carry around, or didn't block much. I designed a folding chipboard divider in Fusion 360 that collapses flat, then built a prototype and stress-tested it. The testing turned up some weak points, and I fixed those in the next version.

Read the full write-up Folding Divider for Limiting Distractions and Increasing the Span of Useful Study Area
06

Rivalry Split Table

Woodworking, CAD-measured layout, painting

A project outside of class. I built a tabletop split down the middle, half Michigan State green with a block S and half Michigan navy with a block M, and the legs match their sides. I planned the split, primed and painted both halves, and hand-painted the legs. I didn't use stencils for the logos because one that size wasn't practical to get. Instead I sized both logos in CAD, worked out the angles and dimensions, and used those measurements to draw each logo freehand on the table before painting it in.

NFL Field Goal Success by Distance

Logistic regression model built on 2024 season data

I wanted to know how much kick distance changes the odds of making a field goal. I took 2024 NFL season data and fit a logistic model to it, worked out a physical upper limit on kick distance using projectile motion, and then checked the model against one kicker's season.
