Projects

01

Drill Press Vice Model

Fusion 360, motion joints, stress simulation

Working from a full set of engineering design sheets, I modeled a drill press vice at true 1:1 scale in Fusion 360. It's built from more than 20 components, assembled with functioning motion joints, and I ran stress simulations afterward to see how it would hold up under real load. Accuracy was the hard part. With that many parts, small dimensional errors stacked up fast, and I ended up rebuilding several components from scratch once tolerances stopped lining up.

02

CNC Sign Design

Fusion 360, CNC toolpathing

My first project involving CNC machining. I designed a wooden nameplate sign and had to program the toolpath myself, an adaptive-clearing and 3D-contour combination I worked out through trial and error, having never toolpathed before. The design changed mid-process too. Once I started modeling, I realized my sketched concept wouldn't translate well to the router, so I redesigned it on the fly and machined the new version instead.

03

Workshop Small-Parts Organizer

Fusion 360, 3D printing (PLA)

I walked through my classroom's workshop looking for a real problem to solve and landed on a common one, small parts left scattered across worktables with nowhere to go. I designed a mountable parts organizer that clips onto a table corner, modeled it in Fusion 360, and 3D printed it in PLA. It's still in use in my own room.

04

Cardboard Chair Design

Partner project · Fusion 360, anthropometric data, load testing

My partner and I set out to build a chair that could hold real weight using nothing but cardboard. Before modeling anything, we collected anthropometric measurements from classmates so the dimensions would actually fit an average student, not just look right on screen. We modeled the design in Fusion 360, split the physical build (one of us cutting, one assembling), and load-tested it to failure. It held 450 lbs before it gave out.

05

Study Divider Design

Fusion 360, user research, iterative prototyping

I wanted to fix a problem I deal with myself, losing focus in shared study spaces. Before designing anything, I surveyed AP and IB students at my school and found nearly three quarters reported being often or always distracted while studying. I looked at what already existed and found the options were either too expensive, too bulky to carry, or didn't block enough. I modeled a folding chipboard desk divider in Fusion 360 that collapses flat when not in use, then built and stress-tested a physical prototype, which surfaced weak points I fixed in the next iteration.

06

Rivalry Split Table

Woodworking, CAD-measured layout, painting

A build outside of class, a tabletop split straight down the middle, half Michigan State green with the block S, half Michigan navy with the block M, legs painted to match each side. I planned the split layout, primed and painted both halves in team colors, then hand-painted the legs. The logos aren't stenciled. A stencil that size wasn't practical to source, so I sized both marks in CAD first, worked out the actual angles and dimensions, and used those measurements to draw each logo directly onto the surface freehand before painting it in.

NFL Field Goal Success by Distance

Logistic regression model built on 2024 season data

A statistical analysis of how field goal success probability changes with kick distance. I pulled 2024 season data, fit a logistic model to it, derived a physical upper bound on kick distance from projectile motion, and checked the model against a real kicker's season stats.
